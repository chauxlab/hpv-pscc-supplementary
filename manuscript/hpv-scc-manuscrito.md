Review

**The Immune Microenvironment in Penile Squamous Cell Carcinoma: Distinctions Between HPV-Driven and HPV-Independent Pathways**

Paola Britos ^1^ and Alcides Chaux ^1,2,\*^

Affiliation: ^1^ ChauxLab Institute, Asunción, Paraguay

^2^ Universidad Central del Paraguay, Ciudad del Este, Paraguay

\* Correspondence: alcides.chaux@central.edu.py; alcides.chaux@chauxlab.com

**Abstract:** **Background:** Penile squamous cell carcinoma (pSCC) is rare yet clinically consequential, with human papillomavirus (HPV)–driven and HPV-independent pathways that may shape the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). Whether these pathways yield reproducible immune distinctions with prognostic or immunotherapy relevance remains incompletely synthesized. **Methods:** We conducted an integrative review (Whittemore and Knafl) reported according to PRISMA 2020. Structured searches of Europe PMC and MEDLINE (13 August 2026), plus an authors' curated bibliography, yielded 318 unique records after deduplication. Title/abstract and full-text screening were performed by two independent reviewers with consensus resolution; data for the 24 sources cited individually in the synthesis were extracted and appraised (Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool, MMAT v.2018) independently by two reviewers and reconciled by consensus, except reference 12, extracted and appraised by P.B. alone because A.C. is a co-author of that study. Synthesis was narrative across TIME composition, HPV± distinctions, prognosis, and immunotherapy; no quantitative meta-analysis across heterogeneous assays or regimens was performed. **Results:** Of 318 screened records, 168 were included at title/abstract; 123 full texts were retrieved (45 unavailable) and 121 reports were included after full-text assessment (2 excluded), corresponding to 119 unique studies (two studies were each reported in both a preprint and a subsequent peer-reviewed publication). Among included reports, original research predominated (n = 85); HPV stratification was available in 80/121. PD-L1 (n = 86), PD-1 (n = 61), and CD8 (n = 47) were the most frequent TIME markers. The most reproducible HPV–TIME contrast was higher PD-L1 in HPV-independent tumors; HPV-positive tumors more often showed selected effector, myeloid, or tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) features, but unsupervised immunophenotypes were not reducible to HPV status alone. Diffuse or high tumor PD-L1, low lymphocytic infiltrate, adverse myeloid spatial patterns, and elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) recurred as adverse signals. In real-world immune-checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) series, the objective response rate was 13% overall and 35% in lymph-node–only metastases (median overall survival 9.8 months; progression-free survival 3.2 months). **Conclusions:** HPV defines a biological axis within pSCC TIME—most clearly PD-L1 enrichment in HPV-independent disease—but is not a complete immune classifier. ICI activity in advanced pSCC is clinically meaningful yet uncommon; biomarker-guided selection remains hypothesis-generating.

**Keywords:** penile squamous cell carcinoma; human papillomavirus; tumor immune microenvironment; PD-L1; immunotherapy; tertiary lymphoid structures; integrative review

# 1. Introduction

Penile cancer is uncommon in high-resource settings but carries a disproportionate burden in regions with limited access to early diagnosis, human papillomavirus (HPV) prevention, circumcision programs, and specialized surgical care, producing marked global disparities in incidence, stage at presentation, and survival (1). Even within Europe, population-based series document gradually rising incidence over recent decades, together with organizational efforts to centralize rare-tumor pathways in high-volume centers (2). These epidemiologic patterns frame pSCC not as a static niche malignancy but as a disease whose outcomes depend heavily on health-system capacity as well as tumor biology.

Approximately 40% of penile tumors are associated with HPV infection in clinical syntheses (3), and large pathology-based cohorts report high-risk HPV DNA in about half of penile squamous cell carcinomas (overall HPV prevalence 54.5%; hrHPV 50.4% in a nationwide Danish series of 1040 cases spanning 1995–2020, without a substantial temporal decline in hrHPV positivity) (4). HPV16 predominates among detected genotypes, reinforcing the prevention relevance of nonavalent vaccination for a sizable fraction of tumors (4). At the same time, a substantial HPV-independent fraction remains, so the pathway dichotomy is biologically and clinically real rather than merely taxonomic.

Contemporary collaborative guidance emphasizes that HPV is a major etiologic risk factor and that pathology reporting should include HPV status assessment as part of the routine diagnostic work-up (5). The 2026 European Association of Urology–American Society of Clinical Oncology (EAU–ASCO) update requires p16 immunohistochemistry in the pathological evaluation of penile carcinoma—with direct molecular HPV testing regarded as desirable but largely research-based—and further expands algorithms for nodal staging and systemic therapy, while acknowledging that many recommendations still rest on retrospective series and expert consensus given the rarity of randomized evidence (6). Within this clinical frame, attention has turned to the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME)—programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression, cytotoxic and regulatory T-cell infiltrates, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), alternative checkpoints, and architectural motifs such as tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS)—as a potential bridge between viral oncogenesis, prognosis, and immunotherapy eligibility.

Existing narrative accounts often mix primary cohorts, evidence syntheses, and case-level responses without explicit stratification by study design and assay compartment. An integrative review that combines heterogeneous empirical designs under a documented multi-database search, independent screening, structured appraisal, and narrative synthesis is therefore needed to clarify which HPV–TIME contrasts are reproducible, which prognostic immune signals are cut-off– and compartment-dependent, and what can—and cannot—be claimed about immune-checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) selection in advanced disease.

The objective of this integrative review was to synthesize evidence on how TIME composition differs between HPV-driven and HPV-independent pSCC, and to summarize implications for prognosis and immunotherapy response. Secondary aims were to map which immune signals are reproducible across study designs, to identify assay and reporting gaps that limit clinical translation, and to state explicitly where the evidence remains hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing.

# 2. Materials and Methods

## 2.1. Design and registration

We performed an integrative review of the TIME in human pSCC, comparing HPV-driven and HPV-independent pathways and summarizing prognostic and immunotherapy-related findings, following the methodological principles of Whittemore and Knafl for combining diverse empirical designs within a single synthesis (7). Reporting followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement (8), adapted to a narrative synthesis; the completed PRISMA 2020 checklist is provided as Supplementary File S3. The review was not prospectively registered, as PROSPERO does not accept integrative reviews; the a priori design elements (eligibility criteria, search channels, synthesis strata, and appraisal tool) are those set out in Sections 2.2–2.6. Certainty judgments are narrative by domain (TIME composition, HPV± contrasts, prognosis, immunotherapy) and do not use a GRADE–RoB2 grid, consistent with the mixed-methods and assay-heterogeneous nature of the corpus. Throughout, "HPV-driven" and "HPV-independent" designate the primary etiologic dichotomy; source-specific terms carried over from included studies (HPV-positive/negative, hrHPV-positive/negative, HPV-associated) are treated as synonymous with this dichotomy unless a study explicitly distinguishes low- from high-risk genotypes, which is noted where relevant.

## 2.2. Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies of any design—including observational cohorts, trials, case series/reports, and reviews used for contextualization—reported TIME composition and/or HPV status in human pSCC with at least one of: CD8 or tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density, PD-L1, regulatory T cells (Tregs), TAMs, other immune checkpoints, immunotherapy response, or prognostic immune associations. English or Spanish publications were eligible without date restriction. Non-human studies (including equine penile SCC models) were excluded. Methodological citations used solely to support Methods (reporting or appraisal frameworks) were not treated as evidence candidates for inclusion/exclusion decisions.

## 2.3. Information sources and search

Structured searches were executed on 13 August 2026 in Europe PMC and MEDLINE. Complementary Boolean strategies combined penile carcinoma terms with HPV vocabulary, TIME/immune-infiltrate descriptors, checkpoint markers (including PD-L1, PD-1, CD8, Treg/FOXP3, TAM/CD163, CTLA-4, and related checkpoints), and immunotherapy or trial identifiers (including PERICLES and HERCULES). Full database-specific Boolean strings are reported verbatim in Supplementary File S1. A bibliography curated by the authors contributed 23 additional pSCC-relevant records, screened as "identified by other methods" under the same eligibility rules and without automatic inclusion (methodological citations for reporting or appraisal frameworks were not counted as candidate records). After within-source and between-source deduplication by DOI/PMID—including preprint-to-published substitutions where a peer-reviewed version was verified—318 unique records entered title/abstract screening.

A complementary contextual search focused on epidemiology, disease burden, and clinical guidelines (2016–August 2026) informed the Introduction and Discussion only; those sources were not added to the PRISMA evidence flow for included studies.

## 2.4. Study selection

Two reviewers screened titles and abstracts independently. Conflicts and "maybe" decisions were resolved by consensus, yielding 168 included and 150 excluded records at title/abstract. Full texts were sought for all consensus-included records: 123 were retrieved and 45 remained unavailable after documented best-effort closure. Full-text screening of the retrieved reports yielded 121 included and 2 excluded reports, corresponding to 119 unique studies (Section 3.1). The complete selection process is summarized in the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (Figure 1).

## 2.5. Data extraction and critical appraisal

Data were extracted into a wide-form template for all 121 included reports. The 24 sources that are cited individually in the Results and tabulated in Tables 1, 2, and 4 were extracted independently by both authors from the full texts and reconciled field by field, with discrepancies resolved by consensus against the source articles, except reference 12 (see Conflict of interest), which was extracted by P.B. alone; the remaining reports were extracted by one author with quality-control checks. Findings used for the synthesis were taken from the full text of core empirical studies, key trials, and selected evidence syntheses. Empirical studies were appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) v.2018 (9); reviews and evidence syntheses were coded not applicable. For the same 24 sources, the MMAT category and criteria c1–c5 were also assigned independently by both authors, with a written rationale for each criterion, and reconciled by consensus, again except reference 12, appraised by P.B. alone. Appraisal is interpreted narratively alongside design, sample size, HPV-method reporting, and assay heterogeneity.

## 2.6. Evidence synthesis

Narrative synthesis was stratified a priori by (1) TIME composition, (2) HPV-driven versus HPV-independent distinctions, (3) prognosis, and (4) immunotherapy. Primary evidence (observational cohorts, trials, case series and reports) supplied all study-level findings and the design, marker, and MMAT counts; systematic reviews and meta-analyses were used only to provide context and to attribute previously published pooled estimates to their original synthesis. No aggregate participant total was computed across the corpus. We did not pool effect estimates across heterogeneous immunohistochemistry (IHC) clones or cut-offs, tissue compartments (tumor versus stroma; intratumoral versus peritumoral), HPV assays (p16, PCR, in situ hybridization), or ICI regimens and lines of therapy. All 119 unique included studies informed the narrative synthesis and the aggregate counts reported in the Results (publication type, marker frequency, MMAT distribution), while a subset of 24 primary sources were selected for individual in-text citation and tabulation in Table 1, applying the following criteria: (a) all trials and basket-trial subgroups reporting pSCC-specific immunotherapy outcomes; (b) cohorts of at least 50 patients reporting a TIME marker together with an HPV or survival association; (c) all cohorts explicitly stratifying TIME findings by HPV status; and (d) evidence syntheses (meta-analyses or systematic/narrative reviews) providing a pooled or synthesized PD-L1– or immunotherapy-outcome estimate. Sources meeting none of these criteria (typically smaller cohorts, case reports, or reviews without a synthesized pSCC-specific estimate) contribute only to the aggregate counts in Section 3.1 and are listed with their key characteristics in Supplementary Table S4. The complete list of 119 unique included studies, with citations, is provided in Supplementary Table S2.

# 3. Results

## 3.1. Study selection and corpus characteristics

Identification recovered 437 records from Europe PMC and 307 from MEDLINE, plus 23 eligible records identified by other methods from a bibliography curated by the authors (767 before between-source deduplication). After deduplication and preprint-to-published substitutions (449 records removed), 318 unique records remained for title/abstract screening. Screening produced 168 included and 150 excluded records; records were excluded for wrong outcome (n = 97), wrong population (n = 41), or wrong publication type (n = 12). Full texts were sought for all 168 consensus-included reports; 123 were retrieved and 45 could not be retrieved after best-effort closure. Among the 123 full texts assessed, 121 were included and 2 were excluded—both broad genitourinary immunotherapy reviews without sufficient pSCC-specific content (wrong population; Thana and Wood, 2020, doi:10.3747/co.27.5121; Khalid et al., 2025, doi:10.3390/curroncol32020108). During data extraction, two of the 121 included reports were found to be preprint versions of other included reports on the same underlying study; the peer-reviewed publication was retained as the citable record in each pair, giving 119 unique studies. The complete flow is shown in Figure 1.

Publication types among the 121 included reports (119 unique studies) were: original research 85, case reports 12, narrative reviews 9, trials 6, meta-analyses 4, systematic reviews 4, and case series 1. This mix deliberately retains evidence syntheses and trial reports for contextualization while privileging primary empirics for TIME contrasts. HPV stratification was reported in 80/121 (yes 76; partial 4). The most frequent TIME markers were PD-L1 (86), PD-1 (61), CD8 (47), TIL (25), FOXP3 (16), and CTLA-4 (16), with lower frequencies for TAM/CD163/CD68, TLS, and alternative checkpoints. MMAT overall ratings were low 84, moderate 19, and not applicable 18 (reviews), indicating that most primary evidence is retrospective and assay-heterogeneous; among the 24 sources cited individually the distribution was moderate 4, low 16, and not applicable 4, with none rated high. Study-level characteristics and key findings for these sources are given in Table 1.

## 3.2. Quality appraisal

The MMAT category and overall judgment for the 24 sources cited individually in the Results are reported per study in Table 4. All were non-randomized: retrospective comparative cohorts or single-arm trials. No randomized comparison of TIME features, or of immunotherapy outcomes by HPV status, has been reported in pSCC.

## 3.3. TIME composition

Across medium-to-large cohorts, pSCC TIME was heterogeneous but repeatedly measurable on archival tissue. Ottenhof et al. profiled PD-L1, CD8, Treg, CD163 macrophages, and HLA class I in 213 tumors, establishing multiplex immune contexture as feasible and prognostically informative in a single high-volume pathway (10). Chu et al. derived unsupervised immunophenotypes from checkpoints and TIL/TAM markers in 178 patients, showing that immune architecture can be classified beyond single-marker positivity and that phenotype classes relate to nodal risk and disease-specific outcomes (11). Canete-Portillo et al. quantified correlated PD-L1, FOXP3, and CD8 densities in tumor and stroma on tissue microarrays (n = 108), linking higher PD-L1/CD8 signals to higher histologic grade and reinforcing coordinated checkpoint–effector–regulatory axes within the same microenvironment (12).

Spatial biology adds a second layer of resolution. Myeloid mapping of CD14/CD68/CD163 distinguished intratumoral versus peritumoral compartments (n = 103) (13), and multiplex immunofluorescence linked higher M1 and helper-T densities to better overall survival while close M2–tumor clustering tracked worse survival and recurrence metrics (n = 57) (14). Newer cohorts highlight TLS-related immunity and FOXP3/CD8 contexture, particularly in HPV-associated disease. Single-cell RNA sequencing of 23 tumors with immunohistochemical validation in 85 patients found that among immune subsets only tumor-infiltrating B cells and TLS differed by HPV status, with HPV-positive tumors showing higher B-cell and plasma-cell content, higher TLS density, and a B cell–IgA–epithelial (polymeric immunoglobulin receptor) axis associated with better survival (15). A tissue-microarray cohort of 83–93 patients likewise found tumor-infiltrating plasma-cell (CD138+) density to be an independent favorable prognostic factor and folded TLS metrics into a prognostic model (16,26). Classic and nonclassic HLA class I downregulation remains a recurrent immune-evasion phenotype in tissue microarray work (n = 168) (17). Semiquantitative clinicopathologic series likewise show high PD-L1 positivity rates among stained subsets, supporting the biologic prevalence of the PD-1/PD-L1 axis even when survival associations are null in smaller samples (18).

Taken together, the corpus supports moderate narrative certainty that PD-L1 and T-cell/myeloid infiltrates are informative TIME axes in pSCC; low–moderate certainty for spatial and TLS metrics (fewer, newer studies); and low certainty for TIM-3, the only checkpoint beyond PD-(L)1/CD8/CTLA-4 assessed among the 24 sources cited individually here (24); other checkpoints (e.g., TIGIT, IDO1) appear only in isolated reports within the broader 119-study corpus, which this narrative synthesis did not further characterize marker-by-marker, and no LAG-3–specific source was identified.

## 3.4. HPV-driven versus HPV-independent distinctions

The most reproducible HPV–TIME contrast was higher PD-L1 in HPV-independent tumors in multiplex cohorts (10), aligned with meta-analytic signals of lower PD-L1 positivity in HPV-positive versus HPV-negative disease (19) and high pooled PD-L1 prevalence with HPV-negative enrichment in updated evidence syntheses (20). In practical terms, HPV-independent tumors more often show higher tumor PD-L1 expression by IHC, whereas HPV-associated tumors are less uniformly PD-L1-high; this describes IHC prevalence, not validated eligibility for immune-checkpoint blockade.

HPV-positive tumors more often showed selected effector or inflamed niches—higher stromal granzyme B and intratumoral PD-1 in hrHPV-positive disease (11), higher intratumoral myeloid densities in hrHPV-positive tumors (13), and TLS-linked antitumor architecture in HPV-associated cohorts (15). Critically, unsupervised immunophenotypes were not overall associated with hrHPV status in the Chu classification, demonstrating that global immune classes are not reducible to a binary HPV split (11). HLA–survival associations concentrated in HPV-negative disease, including independent adverse effects of partial HLA-A loss (17). In spatial macrophage/helper-T analyses, survival differences by HPV were attenuated when helper-T clustering was high, suggesting that local immune geography can dominate viral pathway labels for prognosis (14). Genomic and immune framing in advanced cohorts similarly treats HPV-related biology as one axis among PD-L1, T-cell infiltration, and tumor mutational features rather than a sole classifier (21).

Certainty is moderate for PD-L1 enrichment in HPV-independent pSCC; low–moderate for effector/TLS enrichment in HPV-positive disease; and low for any single dichotomous "immunologically hot versus cold" HPV taxonomy. ## 3.5. Prognosis

Recurring adverse TIME-linked signals included diffuse or high tumor PD-L1 with worse disease-specific outcomes and lymph-node metastasis risk in large cohorts (10,11,22); hrHPV negativity co-traveling with adverse immune profiles in multivariable models (10); low lymphocytic infiltrate and high tumour budding outperforming p16 alone for overall and cancer-specific survival discrimination (23); adverse myeloid/M2 spatial clustering with favorable M1/helper-T density (13,14); elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) in multicenter data (n = 582) (24) and in ICI-treated real-world models (25); and partial HLA-A loss, especially in HPV-negative tumors (17). TLS-related features and composite immune architecture also tracked outcomes in contemporary pathology cohorts (16,26).

Favorable signals mirrored the inverse pattern: denser effector infiltrates, M1/helper-T–rich niches, and TLS-associated architecture. In the immunotherapy setting, HPV positivity was associated with more favorable outcomes among treated patients: in a first-line PD-1–inhibitor combination cohort, HPV-positive status was associated with better response, progression-free survival, and overall survival, retaining significance in that study's multivariable model (27), and in a single-arm chemoimmunotherapy trial HPV16-positive patients had a numerically higher response rate (55.6% versus 35%) and longer progression-free survival than HPV16-negative patients (31). These are associations with outcome among treated patients; without comparator arms they cannot establish that HPV status predicts a treatment-specific benefit. Because IHC clones, scoring compartments (tumor cells versus immune cells; diffuse versus marginal PD-L1), and HPV methods differ across studies, cut-offs are not interchangeable and hazard ratios should not be pooled. Narrative certainty is moderate for adverse diffuse PD-L1 and for NLR as a simple systemic marker; low–moderate for composite immunoscores, TLS, spatial metrics, and for HPV as a treatment-outcome modifier (few, small, non-randomized cohorts without comparator arms).

## 3.6. Immunotherapy

Real-world ICI activity in advanced pSCC is clinically meaningful but uncommon. In the Global Society of Rare Genitourinary Tumors multicenter series—a real-world cohort treated with a mix of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 monotherapy and ipilimumab-containing combinations—the objective response rate was 13% (11/85) overall and 35% (7/20) in lymph-node–only metastases, with median overall survival 9.8 months and progression-free survival 3.2 months; visceral metastases, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≥ 1, and higher NLR were associated with worse survival, whereas HPV status was not: among the 49 patients with HPV data, no OS or PFS difference was observed by HPV status (25). This null finding tempers the HPV-linked associations reported in trial settings below, which largely reflect combination regimens rather than the mixed real-world monotherapy/combination population of this series. Host inflammation indexed by NLR likewise tracked poorer chemotherapy and PD-1 signals in larger multicenter data, linking systemic myeloid bias to treatment failure phenotypes (24). Observational PD-1–treated cohorts suggest HPV may modify outcomes, but confirmatory prospective evidence remains thin (27). A systematic review of immunotherapy in penile cancer management concludes that activity is real yet limited and that biomarker selection remains immature (28).

Trial and basket settings support continued investigation rather than routine biomarker-gated prescribing. In the phase I cabozantinib–nivolumab–ipilimumab program the penile subgroup was small (9 evaluable) but showed a 44% response rate with a disease control rate of 100% (29), whereas the penile cohort of the pembrolizumab–vorinostat basket trial did not meet its response endpoint (18%, 2/11) and had the shortest overall survival of the six squamous-carcinoma cohorts (30). The nonrandomized HERCULES (LACOG 0218) trial of first-line pembrolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy in advanced penile cancer reported a 39.4% investigator-assessed response rate and median overall survival of 9.6 months (31). Exploratory neoadjuvant and perioperative PD-1 strategies report radiologic responses and occasional pathologic complete responses in small series, without robust prediction by PD-L1 combined positive score thresholds (32). Perspectives on anti–PD-1 activity in genital squamous contexts, including cemiplimab experience in cutaneous squamous disease, further frame rare genital SCC as immunotherapy-relevant but still evidence-thin (33). The single-center, nonrandomized PERICLES phase II trial of atezolizumab, with or without radiotherapy, in 32 patients with advanced pSCC did not meet its primary 1-year progression-free survival endpoint (12.5%; 95% CI, 5.0–31.3); exploratory analyses found high-risk HPV positivity and high intratumoral CD3+CD8+ infiltration associated with longer progression-free survival, associations that a single-arm, non-comparator design cannot establish as predictive of treatment benefit (34).

Across layers, HPV and PD-L1 supply enrichment hypotheses—especially HPV-independent / PD-L1-high phenotypes—but do not constitute validated predictive rules for routine ICI selection. Certainty is moderate for modest ORR with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy in advanced disease, whether given alone or in the ipilimumab-, chemotherapy-, or HDAC-inhibitor-containing combinations used in the trials above, and response rates are not directly comparable across these regimens; certainty is low for biomarker-guided selection in current practice. Case reports document striking individual responses but were not used to estimate class-level effect sizes.

# 4. Discussion

This integrative review of 121 full-text–included reports (119 unique studies) supports three cross-cutting conclusions. First, HPV is a biological axis within pSCC TIME rather than a complete immune classifier: PD-L1 enrichment in HPV-independent tumors is the clearest repeated contrast across primary cohorts and evidence syntheses (10,19,20), whereas effector, myeloid, and TLS features often favor HPV-positive disease with substantial overlap and non-redundant unsupervised phenotypes (11,13–15). Second, compartment and pattern—diffuse versus marginal PD-L1, intratumoral versus peritumoral myeloid density, spatial M2 clustering—carry more prognostic information than binary marker positivity alone (10,13,14,23). Third, systemic host inflammation indexed by NLR bridges tissue immunology and treatment outcomes in both general advanced disease and ICI-treated cohorts, implying that host state may matter as much as single-lesion PD-L1 for clinical decision-making (24,25).

These findings align with collaborative guidelines on HPV assessment in pathology: the 2026 EAU–ASCO update requires p16 immunohistochemistry as part of the pathological evaluation of penile carcinoma, while stating that direct molecular confirmation of HPV is desirable but is mainly performed in research settings (6); the 2023 collaborative guideline likewise embeds HPV status in routine reporting (5). Both documents caution that advanced-disease recommendations remain constrained by limited prospective evidence (5,6). Rising incidence and centralization trends in some health systems further underscore the need for reproducible biomarkers that travel across laboratories and referral networks (2). Global disparity reviews remind readers that TIME biology will matter most where access to organ-preserving surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy is uneven, so translational claims must remain modest until assay standardization and equitable trial access improve (1). Mechanistically, HPV-associated tumors may more often recruit organized lymphoid niches, whereas HPV-independent tumors more often show higher PD-L1 expression by IHC; both patterns are compatible with partial responses to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade without implying a universal selection rule, and neither has been validated as a predictive biomarker for treatment selection (3,10,15,25,28). For clinicians, the practical message is therefore cautious: HPV status and PD-L1 IHC can enrich biological hypotheses and trial stratification, but they should not yet be treated as exclusionary or mandatory predictive tests for offering ICI in advanced pSCC outside a research context. For pathologists, the priority remains standardized HPV reporting and transparent documentation of PD-L1 clone, compartment, and pattern, because those methodological details determine whether published associations can be reproduced in local practice (5,10,12,20).

The strength of these conclusions is bounded by the methodological quality of the underlying evidence. Among the 24 sources that carry the synthesis, MMAT appraisal rated 16 as low and 4 as moderate, with none high; all empirical designs were non-randomized comparative cohorts or single-arm trials. Several recurring weaknesses were identified at the criterion level. First, tissue-availability selection was near-universal: cohorts were assembled from archival blocks with adequate material, often without a stated consecutive-ascertainment rule, so representativeness of the broader pSCC population cannot be assumed. Second, in three of the marker-based cohorts the low/high cut-points for prognostic markers were optimized against each study's own survival outcome, a circularity that can inflate the apparent strength of the resulting associations (10,11,26). Third, some of the most-cited cohorts are not independent of one another: two of the Netherlands series draw on the same institutional 2001–2009 resection archive (10,13), so their concordant HPV–myeloid and HPV–survival signals should be read as one body of evidence rather than two. Fourth, one correlative study was analyzed predominantly at the tissue-microarray-spot level (up to 528 spots from 108 patients) without adjustment for multiple correlated spots per patient, and inferred HPV status from morphology rather than molecular testing (12). These features do not overturn the main contrasts, which recur across independent groups, methods, and countries, but they place a firm ceiling on certainty and reinforce the hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing character of the synthesis.

Limitations are inherent to the evidence and to this review design. Forty-five full texts sought after title/abstract inclusion could not be retrieved (titles, years, and DOIs are listed in the open repository, see Data availability); 25/45 (56%) were published in 2023 or later and 17/45 (38%) had an immunotherapy- or checkpoint-related term in the title, so retrieval bias, if present, most plausibly affects recent immunotherapy evidence rather than the older TIME-composition literature that anchors most of Table 1. HPV assay methods were incompletely reported in a large fraction of the included studies, so HPV± contrasts should be read as pathway labels rather than uniformly verified viral classification across the entire corpus. MMAT ratings were frequently low or indeterminate, reflecting retrospective designs, modest samples, and heterogeneous endpoints; appraisal is interpreted narratively rather than as a definitive risk-of-bias league table. Screening was independent for all records, and data extraction and appraisal were independent for the 24 sources that carry the synthesis, except reference 12, extracted and appraised by one author alone because of a co-authorship conflict (see Conflict of interest); extraction of the remaining reports was performed by one author with quality-control checks rather than in duplicate. No quantitative meta-analysis was appropriate across IHC cut-offs, compartments, or ICI regimens. Two preprint/publication pairs among the 121 included reports were identified during data extraction and merged, giving 119 unique studies. The review was not registered. Case reports and small series illustrate dramatic responses but must not drive effect estimates. Finally, authorship overlap of the investigators with one included primary study (reference 12) is a potential conflict; that source was extracted and appraised by the author with no role in it.

# 5. Conclusions

HPV-driven and HPV-independent pSCC share a measurable but heterogeneous TIME, with the most reproducible distinction being PD-L1 enrichment in HPV-independent tumors and more variable effector/TLS features in HPV-associated disease. ICI monotherapy yields clinically meaningful yet uncommon responses in advanced pSCC; disease distribution and host inflammation appear as important as single-marker PD-L1. Prospective trials that co-register standardized HPV methods, compartment-aware immune assays, and clinical endpoints are required before TIME biomarkers can guide therapy.
